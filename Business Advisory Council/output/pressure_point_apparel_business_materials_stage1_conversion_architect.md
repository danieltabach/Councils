## Full CRO & Funnel Audit | June 2026

## Preliminary Orientation

Before diving into each section: this audit is being conducted on a brand with approximately 39 total sales, no paid traffic, ~300 Instagram followers, and a site that is live but not yet battle-tested at volume. That context matters enormously. At this stage, the conversion problem is not primarily *on-site friction* — it's a **traffic and trust problem**. You cannot meaningfully optimize a checkout flow that sees 3 visitors a day. However, the site has real, fixable issues that will matter the moment traffic scales, and some of them are costing sales *right now*. I will call both categories out clearly and tell you which to fix today versus which to fix at 500 monthly visitors.

I've reviewed all screenshots, financial data, brand materials, and the full founder brief. Here is my complete assessment.

## Customer Journey Map

### The Full Funnel — Where It Stands Today

**Stage 1: Awareness**
*How people find out Pressure Point exists*

Current sources:
- Instagram organic (~300 followers, 3-4 posts/week)
- Word of mouth from gifted product in local gym community
- Direct referrals from Rosa and Daniel's personal networks
- Zero paid discovery
- Zero TikTok presence
- Zero SEO-driven discovery (no blog content, no keyword strategy)

**Leak assessment: CRITICAL.** The awareness funnel is nearly closed. The brand is essentially invisible outside of a small Instagram following and a tight local gym circle. 39 sales in 4 months from a network of people who already know Rosa is not a conversion problem — it's an audience size problem. The funnel cannot convert what it never receives.

**Stage 2: Consideration**
*First visit to the site — does the brand earn attention?*

A visitor who finds the site via Instagram or word-of-mouth arrives with some prior context. The brand story is compelling. The site communicates identity clearly. The "Our Origin" page is well-written and emotionally resonant.

However, a cold visitor — someone who stumbles onto the site with zero prior brand exposure — faces a trust gap. There are no reviews. No social proof beyond a few product photos. No UGC. No press. No community signal. For a brand with no name recognition, this is a significant conversion barrier.

**Leak assessment: HIGH.** Trust infrastructure is thin. A cold visitor has no third-party validation to anchor their consideration.

**Stage 3: First Visit / Browse**
*Navigation, product discovery, and intent signals*

The site is navigable. The drop structure (Drop 001, Drop 002) creates a logical browsing path. The "Start Here" collection is a smart orientation mechanism. Product photography on the rashguard and fight shorts is noticeably stronger than the tee photography (more on this below).

The biggest browse-stage issue: **the site feels like it's on sale before it's earned the right to be.** Every single product is marked "Sale" with a crossed-out original price. This is a trust-eroding pattern. It signals either that the original price was artificial, or that the brand is struggling to move inventory. Neither reading helps conversion.

**Leak assessment: MODERATE-HIGH.** Sale pricing across the entire catalog undermines the mid-premium positioning the brand is trying to own.

**Stage 4: Add to Cart**
*Product page to cart conversion*

Product pages are functional and have solid copy. The "You May Also Like" section is active. ShopPay installments are visible on higher-ticket items. Size selectors work.

What's missing: no size guide linked from product pages, no reviews, no "X people are looking at this" or scarcity signal, no fabric/fit comparison to help first-time buyers make a confident decision on rashguards or shorts.

**Leak assessment: MODERATE.** The product pages are good for a 4-month-old brand but have specific fixable gaps that are causing hesitation.

**Stage 5: Checkout**
*Cart to completed purchase*

Shopify's native checkout is generally clean and low-friction. ShopPay is available. Multiple payment methods are visible in the footer. The basics are covered.

Unknown: what percentage of add-to-carts are completing checkout. This is a critical unknown. Flag it. Once Shopify order sync is complete in the dashboard, this becomes trackable.

**Leak assessment: UNKNOWN — needs data. Likely moderate friction from shipping cost surprise (more below).**

**Stage 6: Post-Purchase**
*What happens after the first sale*

Post-purchase email is active. That's good. But the welcome sequence doesn't exist, which means new subscribers who don't buy immediately fall into a void. The post-purchase experience appears to be a single email — no sequence, no community invitation, no referral ask, no review request.

**Leak assessment: HIGH.** The post-purchase window is the highest-trust moment in the entire relationship. It's being underutilized.

**Stage 7: Retention & Repeat Purchase**
*Getting customers back*

No win-back sequence. No loyalty mechanism. No SMS. No community structure (Discord, private Instagram group, etc.) that would give repeat buyers a reason to stay engaged between drops.

For a drop-based brand, the repeat purchase cycle depends entirely on the customer knowing when the next drop happens. If they're not on email, they won't know.

**Leak assessment: HIGH.** Retention infrastructure is essentially nonexistent beyond the post-purchase email.

**Stage 8: Referral**
*Customers bringing in new customers*

The gifting strategy to gym community members is the brand's de facto referral program. It's working at a grassroots level. But there's no formal referral mechanism — no "share with a training partner" incentive, no referral code structure, no UGC ask that turns customers into content creators.

**Leak assessment: MODERATE.** The organic referral engine is there but unstructured and unscalable.

## Storefront Conversion Audit

### What I See in the Screenshots

**Homepage (001-home.png)**

The homepage has strong bones. The hero section with "Pressure Is a Privilege / Built for Those Who Persevere" is clear and on-brand. The two CTAs ("Wear Pressure" and "Meet the Brand") give visitors a choice between buying and learning — that's a correct bifurcation for a brand where trust-building is necessary.

The Drop 002 section with the red background creates visual energy and draws the eye. The Drop 001 bundle offer with "20% off when you get the full kit" is a smart AOV play.

**Issues I'm flagging:**

1. **The "Sale" banner problem.** Looking at the product grid (WEBSITE PRICES.png), every single product shows a crossed-out original price and a sale price. Logo Tee: ~~$30~~ $20. Art of Pressure Tee: ~~$30~~ $20. Fight Shorts: ~~$50~~ $45. Rashguard: ~~$55~~ $50. This is a significant brand positioning problem. The brand is trying to own "mid-premium, intentional, small-batch quality" — but the entire catalog is perpetually on sale. Sale pricing works for clearance or urgency events. Running it across 100% of your catalog, indefinitely, trains customers to see the sale price as the real price, makes the original price feel fabricated, and erodes the "quality over gimmicks" identity the brand is building. **Recommendation: Remove sale pricing from the standard catalog. If you want to create urgency, use it for specific drops with a defined end date and communicate the reason.**

2. **No email popup or lead capture on the homepage.** The footer has a static email field ("Don't stop there!"), but there's no popup, exit-intent capture, or proactive lead capture mechanism. For a brand with thin traffic, every visitor who leaves without buying and without subscribing is a permanent loss. The footer form is passive — most visitors won't scroll to it. A timed popup (30 seconds, or on exit intent) offering something specific ("Get early access to Drop 003") would materially increase list growth. This is the highest-ROI fix available at current traffic levels.

3. **The Instagram feed section on the homepage.** I can see what appears to be an embedded Instagram feed section mid-homepage. At 300 followers with inconsistent content quality (by Rosa's own admission), this section may be showing content that doesn't convert. If the Instagram feed is live on the homepage, audit what it's showing. If the most recent posts are philosophy quotes or memes rather than product-on-body content, it's not helping conversion.

4. **Navigation clarity.** "Start Here" as a nav item is clever for a new visitor, but "Shop All" as a dropdown with "Our Origin" and "Reach Out" in the main nav creates a slightly awkward hierarchy. The nav is functional but not optimized. Minor issue at current traffic levels.

**Product Pages (003, 004, 006, 007)**

The product pages are the strongest part of the site. Specific observations:

**What's working:**
- The rashguard page (006) has the most photos (7 thumbnails visible) and the strongest copy. The line "The mat doesn't lie, and neither does your gear" is excellent. The pressure-reveal fabric detail is a compelling differentiator and it's communicated clearly.
- The fight shorts page (007) also has strong multi-angle photography with the red background. The copy is functional and sport-specific.
- "You May Also Like" cross-sell sections are present and populated on all product pages. Good.
- ShopPay installments are visible on the rashguard ($12.50/installment) and shorts ($22.50). This reduces the psychological barrier on higher-ticket items.

**What's missing or broken:**

1. **No size guide.** This is a conversion killer for apparel, especially for a brand with no name recognition. A first-time buyer buying a rashguard at $50 has no idea how this brand's sizing compares to Tatami, Hayabusa, or their Walmart tee. Size uncertainty = abandoned cart. A simple size chart (chest measurement to size) linked from the size selector would reduce this friction immediately. Cost to implement: 30 minutes.

2. **No reviews.** Every product page has zero customer reviews. I understand this is a 4-month-old brand with 39 sales — you don't have a deep review pool. But you have gifted 23 units and given 10 founder samples. That's 33 people wearing the product who could leave reviews. **Reach out to every single one of them and ask for a review this week.** Even 5-10 reviews per product transforms the trust signal for a cold visitor. The Shopify product review app is free.

3. **The tee photography is significantly weaker than the rashguard/shorts photography.** The founder's tee and art of pressure tee are shot on what appears to be a ghost mannequin or flat lay against a smoky gray background. The rashguard and shorts are shot on an actual model against a red background with dynamic posing. The disparity is noticeable. The tees look like Amazon product listings. The rashguard looks like a brand. This is costing tee conversions. **Priority fix: Get tees on a body with the same red-background treatment.**

4. **The "Founder's Tee" naming creates confusion.** Is this a limited founder's edition? Is it the brand's original tee? The name doesn't communicate anything about the product to a new visitor. Consider renaming for clarity or adding a line of context in the product title or description.

5. **No return/exchange policy visible from product pages.** First-time buyers buying apparel online have a core anxiety: "What if it doesn't fit?" The return policy is presumably in the footer (I can see policy links in the News page footer), but it's not surfaced on product pages. A one-line "Free exchanges on size issues" or "Easy returns within 30 days" linked near the Add to Cart button would reduce this anxiety at the decision point.

6. **The "Women's Logo Tee" is in inventory (11 units) but I don't see it prominently featured in any of the collection screenshots.** If it's buried or unlisted, you have 11 units sitting in dead stock. Verify it's live, properly tagged, and appearing in collections.

**Collections Pages (011, 013, 015)**

The "Start Here" collection (011) correctly surfaces the three highest-value products. The Drop 002 collection (013) is clean. The "All Products" page (015) shows 5 products in a grid.

One issue: **the brand copy block that appears at the bottom of every collection page ("Pressure Is a Privilege / Most people run from pressure...") is the same block repeated verbatim across Start Here, Drop 002, and All Products.** This is a missed opportunity. Each collection page should have a brief, specific intro that orients the visitor to that collection's context. The copy block feels like a template placeholder that was never customized.

**Contact Page (008)**

Functional. The "No pressure" line in the subheader is on-brand. No issues here.

**Blog / News Page (014)**

Empty. The blog exists but has no posts. This is a missed SEO and content opportunity, but at current traffic levels it's a lower priority. If Daniel can set up a basic blog structure, Rosa's "Pressure Principles" Instagram content can be repurposed as blog posts with minimal additional effort — building SEO equity over time.

## Checkout Flow Assessment

### What's In Place

Shopify's native checkout is the industry standard for low-friction ecommerce. For a Basic plan, you get:
- Guest checkout (no account creation required — good)
- ShopPay express checkout
- Multiple payment methods (visible in footer: Visa, Mastercard, Amex, PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay, Klarna, and more)
- Automatic address validation

This is solid. The checkout itself is unlikely to be the primary conversion killer.

### The Shipping Cost Problem

This is the most likely checkout abandonment cause that I can identify from the data provided.

The current model: customers pay for shipping, but Rosa absorbs $3.50-$5.00 per order because the actual shipping cost exceeds what customers pay. This means:

**Scenario A:** If customers are paying $2-$7 for shipping (as implied by the zone data), and the actual label cost is $6-$12, then Rosa is subsidizing the difference. This is already happening.

**Scenario B:** If shipping costs are not displayed until checkout, customers who see "free browsing" may hit a $6-$8 shipping charge at checkout and abandon.

The data I need but don't have: what shipping cost does the customer see at checkout, and at what stage in the checkout flow is it displayed? If it's not shown until the payment step, that's a classic abandoned cart trigger.

**Recommendation:** Display estimated shipping cost on the cart page, before checkout begins. Shopify allows this natively. Surprise shipping costs at checkout are the #1 cause of checkout abandonment across all of ecommerce.

**Longer-term recommendation:** Model a free shipping threshold. At current margins, a $75 free shipping threshold would push customers toward buying a rashguard + tee combo (AOV ~$70) to unlock it. The shipping cost Rosa absorbs (~$5) is less than the incremental margin on the additional tee (~$7-13). This is a net-positive AOV play that also eliminates the checkout friction.

### Add-to-Cart to Purchase Rate

This metric is currently unknown. Once Shopify order sync is complete in Daniel's dashboard, this becomes trackable. For context: industry average add-to-cart-to-purchase rate for apparel DTC is approximately 35-45%. For a new brand with no social proof, expect to be lower — possibly 20-30% — until trust signals improve.

**Flag:** The Meta Pixel is now collecting data. Once you have 30+ days of data, you'll be able to see where in the funnel people are dropping off via Meta Events Manager, even without running ads. Check this monthly.
