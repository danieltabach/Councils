# Fact-Check Report — Business Advisory Council Output
## Pressure Point Apparel | June 2026

This document audits verifiable factual claims made in the council's final report (`pressure_point_apparel_business_materials_20260607_031323.md`). Claims are rated from most to least concerning.

---

## HALLUCINATIONS (Statements that are materially wrong)

### 1. "The global combat sports apparel market was valued at approximately $5.2–5.8 billion in 2024"
**Source:** Market Analyst, line ~1012
**Reality:** The global combat sports *products* market (all equipment, not just apparel) was valued at $8.89 billion in 2024 growing to $9.54B in 2025 (CAGR 7.2%). There is no reputable source citing $5.2-5.8B specifically for "combat sports apparel." The model appears to have invented a sub-segment number that doesn't correspond to published research.
**Correct range:** Full market is ~$8.9B. The apparel-only sub-segment is not separately valued in published reports at this level.

Sources:
- [Combat Sports Products Market Report 2025 - Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6035272/combat-sports-products-market-report)
- [Combat Sports Products Market - The Business Research Company](https://www.thebusinessresearchcompany.com/report/combat-sports-products-global-market-report)

### 2. "Estimates place active BJJ practitioners in the United States at approximately 1.5–2 million, with global participation exceeding 10 million"
**Source:** Market Analyst, line ~1017
**Reality:** Most sources estimate US BJJ practitioners at approximately 500,000–750,000, not 1.5-2 million. The "10 million global" figure has no authoritative sourcing. The model inflated participation numbers by roughly 2-3x.
**Correct range:** ~500K-750K US practitioners, based on studio counts (44,218 studios in 2024) and participation surveys.

Sources:
- [BJJ Statistics: Jiu Jitsu by the Numbers - Gold BJJ](https://goldbjj.com/blogs/roll/statistics)
- [How Many People Train BJJ? - Grapplers Graveyard](https://grapplersgraveyard.com/how-many-people-train-jiu-jitsu/)
- [100+ Jiu Jitsu Statistics | Sourced 2026](https://wifitalents.com/jiu-jitsu-statistics/)

### 3. "The North American grappling training apparel segment sits in the $400–600 million range annually"
**Source:** Market Analyst, line ~1015
**Reality:** No published market research supports this specific figure. Given that the full combat sports *products* market is ~$9B globally (including all equipment, all regions), a $400-600M US-only grappling apparel sub-segment seems plausible but is not verified by any cited source. This appears to be model estimation presented as fact.
**Rating:** Unverifiable — possibly reasonable but presented with false confidence.

## MISLEADING (True-ish but framed incorrectly or overstated)

### 4. "No other grappling apparel brand has a formalized, legally committed mental health charity partnership"
**Source:** Strategy Consultant, line ~337; repeated in Deliberation
**Reality:** Multiple BJJ brands already donate to mental health/suicide prevention:
- No Bull Jiu Jitsu: $2 per rashguard to Suicide Prevention Lifeline
- Nation Athletic BJJ: portion of sales to The Trevor Project
- Hyperfly: YCTH Foundation + 10% of September sales to causes + partnership with REORG (veteran mental health/jiu-jitsu charity)

**What IS fair:** None of PPA's direct competitors (Venum, Shoyoroll, Hayabusa, Tatami, Level Black) have a permanent, every-order percentage commitment to a mental health org. The differentiator is real but overstated as "no other grappling brand does this."
**Better framing:** "The only brand in the competitive set with a permanent, every-sale mental health commitment."

Sources:
- [No Bull Jiu Jitsu - Suicide Prevention Rashguard](https://www.amazon.com/No-Bull-Jiu-Jitsu-Prevention/dp/B08RX6Y6MD)
- [Hyperfly YCTH Foundation](https://hyperfly.com/blogs/hyperfly-blog/what-is-the-ycth-foundation-and-who-has-it-helped)
- [Hyperfly x REORG](https://hyperfly.com/blogs/hyperfly-blog/discover-reorg-the-charity-saving-veterans-through-jiu-jitsu)

### 5. "Instagram's algorithm in 2026 does not distribute content to non-followers at meaningful scale unless you are already large or running paid ads"
**Source:** Strategy Consultant, line ~498; Marketing Strategist line ~496
**Reality:** Partially true but overstated. Business accounts do have 47% lower initial reach than creator accounts. Average post reaches only 3.5% of followers (down from 10-15% in 2020). HOWEVER, 55% of all Reel views come from non-followers, making Reels the strongest organic discovery tool. The claim is true for static posts but misleading for Reels content.
**Better framing:** "Instagram heavily favors existing followers for feed posts, but Reels still have meaningful non-follower reach (~55% of views). At 300 followers, the reach ceiling is still very low either way."

Sources:
- [40 Instagram Organic Reach Statistics 2026](https://www.outfame.com/blog/instagram-organic-reach-statistics)
- [Instagram Algorithm 2026 - Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/instagram-algorithm/)

### 6. "A single well-executed TikTok video from a brand with zero followers can reach 50,000 people. That is not a hypothetical — it is the documented mechanics of the platform."
**Source:** Strategy Consultant / Marketing Strategist, line ~498
**Reality:** Technically possible but misleadingly presented as typical. TikTok's 2026 algorithm now tests videos with existing followers FIRST before pushing to non-followers (major 2026 change). Completion rate bar raised to ~70% for viral distribution (up from ~50% in 2024). Zero-follower virality still happens but is harder than implied. A "small hijab business gained 50K followers overnight" is cited as a real case, but this is an outlier, not the norm.
**Better framing:** "TikTok's algorithm CAN surface zero-follower content to large audiences, which is impossible on Instagram. But 2026 changes mean it tests with your followers first. Viral reach is possible but not guaranteed."

Sources:
- [TikTok Algorithm 2026 - Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-algorithm/)
- [TikTok Algorithm Changes 2026 - Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/tiktok-algorithm-2026-what-works-now)
- [TikTok Algorithm 2026 - Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/)

### 7. "Shoyoroll $180+ (resale $400+)"
**Source:** Brand Strategy doc (provided by founders) + repeated by Market Analyst
**Reality:** Shoyoroll gis retail at $250-$265 (not $180). Rashguards are separate products. Resale on gis can indeed exceed $400 for limited editions. The $180+ figure appears to conflate rashguard and gi pricing, or references older pricing. Current Shoyoroll rashguards are in the $60-$80 retail range.
**Rating:** The $180+ likely refers to gis (which start at $250 now, not $180). Resale $400+ is confirmed for limited gis. The claim undersells Shoyoroll's actual price point.

Sources:
- [Shoyoroll Official Store](https://shoyoroll.com/)
- [Shoyoroll on BJJ Gi Seller](https://bjjgiseller.com/collections/vendors?q=shoyoroll)

## MOSTLY TRUE WITH CAVEATS

### 8. "Hidden shipping is the #1 cause of cart abandonment"
**Source:** Chief Advisory Officer synthesis, line ~142
**Reality:** Confirmed. 48-55% of shoppers abandon carts due to unexpected extra costs (shipping, taxes, fees) revealed at checkout. This is consistently the #1 reason in every major study (Baymard Institute, Shopify, etc.). However, it's "extra costs" broadly — not shipping alone. Taxes and fees also contribute.
**Caveat:** More precisely "unexpected extra costs at checkout" rather than specifically "hidden shipping."

Sources:
- [Cart Abandonment Statistics 2026 - DontPayFull](https://www.dontpayfull.com/explore/cart-abandonment-statistics)
- [Extra Costs #1 Reason - eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/extra-costs-are-the-top-reason-consumers-abandon-online-carts)

### 9. "Suicide rates among young men — your exact primary demographic — are a documented crisis"
**Source:** Marketing Strategist, line ~447; Strategy Consultant line ~337
**Reality:** Confirmed with nuance. Suicide rates for men aged 25-34 rose 30% since 2010. Males account for ~80% of all suicides (38,977 male vs 9,847 female in 2024). Suicide is the 2nd leading cause of death for ages 15-34. However, there was a slight 4% decrease from 2023-2024 overall. "Crisis" is editorial but statistically supportable given the long-term 30% increase.
**Caveat:** The trend is real but the very recent year-over-year data shows a slight decline, not acceleration.

Sources:
- [AFSP Suicide Statistics](https://afsp.org/suicide-statistics/)
- [NIMH Suicide Statistics](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/statistics/suicide)
- [Male Suicide Rates - American Institute for Boys and Men](https://aibm.org/research/male-suicide/)

### 10. "This is exactly how Tatami built its early US base" (through gym partnerships and geographic density)
**Source:** Chief Advisory Officer, line ~150; Market Analyst deliberation
**Reality:** Confirmed directionally. Tatami Fightwear was founded in 2009 in South Wales, sponsors "hundreds of martial arts academies," designs/retails/wholesales combat sports products, and built its reputation through grassroots support "at all levels from grassroots upwards." Their About page confirms gym-partnership-driven distribution. However, the specific claim that it was "geographic density" in the US specifically is not documented — Tatami is UK-based and expanded globally.
**Caveat:** Tatami did use gym partnerships as a primary growth channel. The "US base" and "geographic density" specifics are inferred, not documented.

Sources:
- [Tatami Fightwear - About Us](https://www.tatamifightwear.com/pages/about-tatami)
- [Tatami Partnerships](https://www.tatamifightwear.com/collections/partnerships)
- [Tatami x UKBJJA](https://www.tatamifightwear.com/blogs/news/tatami-fightwear-x-ukbjja)

### 11. "Email is typically 30-40% of revenue for ecommerce"
**Source:** Referenced implicitly in marketing strategist section as industry standard
**Reality:** Confirmed. Klaviyo and industry benchmarks cite 20-40% of DTC revenue from email as achievable, with 25-40% recommended for brands with strong repeat purchase dynamics. Sports/recreation has the highest email open rates (~40.6%).
**Caveat:** 30-40% is the aspirational best-in-class range, not the average. Average is closer to 25%.

Sources:
- [Klaviyo 2026 Email Benchmarks](https://www.klaviyo.com/products/email-marketing/benchmarks)
- [Klaviyo Ecommerce Benchmark Report](https://www.klaviyo.com/marketing-resources/ecommerce-benchmarks)

### 12. "Industry average add-to-cart-to-purchase rate for apparel DTC is approximately 35-45%"
**Source:** Conversion Architect, line ~824
**Reality:** This appears to conflate add-to-cart rate with cart-to-purchase rate. Add-to-cart rate for apparel is ~6.3-7.1%. Cart abandonment rate is ~70-75%. So the cart-to-purchase rate (those who add to cart and then buy) is approximately 25-30%, NOT 35-45%. The council's figure is inflated by ~10-15 percentage points.
**Correct range:** ~25-30% of add-to-carts convert to purchase in apparel.

Sources:
- [Add-to-Cart Rate Statistics - Opensend](https://www.opensend.com/post/add-to-cart-rate-statistics-ecommerce)
- [Ecommerce Conversion Rate Benchmarks 2026](https://blendcommerce.com/blogs/shopify/ecommerce-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026)

### 13. "DTC apparel CAC via paid social is typically $25-$60"
**Source:** Market Analyst, line ~1224
**Reality:** Low end of current reality. 2026 data shows DTC fashion brands averaging $45-$75 per customer via paid (Meta + Google), with blended CAC reaching $90-$129 for fashion/accessories. The $25-$60 range is possible for community-driven brands with strong organic but is not the "typical" paid CAC anymore.
**Caveat:** $25-$60 may have been accurate in 2023 but is understated for 2026 Meta costs (CPMs up 89% since 2020).

Sources:
- [DTC Fashion CAC 2026 - Clothing Brands](https://www.clothingbrands.org/dtc-fashion-brand-customer-acquisition-costs/)
- [Average CAC by Ecommerce Vertical 2026 - Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-cac-ecommerce-vertical)
- [Customer Acquisition Cost by Industry - Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/customer-acquisition-cost-by-industry)

### 14. "Nasty Gal... built their initial audience on promotional pricing and then couldn't escape it"
**Source:** Future Visionary deliberation, line ~2410
**Reality:** Confirmed. Nasty Gal was the subject of a class action lawsuit over "fake sale pricing" — inflated original prices to create artificial discounts. The brand collapsed into bankruptcy. Business of Fashion documented multiple strategic failures. The pricing trap comparison is valid.
**Caveat:** Nasty Gal's failure was multi-causal (founder distraction, aggressive expansion, fake pricing lawsuits). Pricing alone didn't kill it but was a significant factor.

Sources:
- [Nasty Gal Class Action - Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/consumer-products/apparel/nasty-gal-class-action-blasts-fake-sale-pricing/)
- [Nasty Gal: What Went Wrong? - Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/news-analysis/nasty-gal-what-went-wrong/)

## UNVERIFIABLE (Reasonable but no source exists)

### 19. "The SAM is likely in the range of 250,000–400,000 potential customers... representing a $22–44 million annual revenue opportunity"
**Source:** Market Analyst, line ~1026-1028
**Rating:** This is a model-constructed estimate (15-20% of US BJJ practitioners × $45-55 AOV × 1-2 purchases/year). The math is internally consistent but depends on the inflated 1.5-2M practitioner base (actual: ~500-750K). Corrected math: 15-20% of 500-750K = 75,000-150,000 customers. At $50 AOV × 1.5 purchases = $5.6M-$11.25M TAM. Still viable for a $2-5M brand, but roughly half the stated opportunity.

### 20. "Your LTV may be 3–5x a normal apparel brand's if you build community"
**Source:** Future Visionary, line ~202/2436
**Rating:** Reasonable hypothesis but unverifiable. Shoyoroll customers do buy every drop at $250+, which would support 3-5x LTV vs. standard apparel. But no published data confirms community-led BJJ apparel LTV multipliers specifically.

## SUMMARY

| Category | Count | Key Takeaways |
|----------|-------|---------------|
| **Hallucinations** | 3 | Market sizing numbers (BJJ practitioners, apparel market size) are inflated 2-3x |
| **Misleading** | 4 | AFSP uniqueness overstated; TikTok/Instagram claims oversimplified; Shoyoroll pricing slightly off |
| **Mostly True w/ Caveats** | 7 | Cart abandonment, suicide stats, Tatami history, email benchmarks, CAC ranges — all directionally correct but nuances matter |
| **Truthful** | 4 | Quiet luxury trend, ALD comparison, CAGR, free shipping impact — all fully verified |

**Bottom line:** The council's strategic advice is sound. The market sizing numbers are the weakest link — inflated by 2-3x on BJJ participation. The AFSP claim needs reframing. All other factual claims are either confirmed or directionally correct with minor caveats. None of the factual errors change the council's core recommendations (gym partnerships first, TikTok second, fix site trust signals, launch welcome email).
