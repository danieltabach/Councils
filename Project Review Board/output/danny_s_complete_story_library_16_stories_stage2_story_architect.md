## Disagreements

**Reviewer A (Assembly Strategist) — dropping SwagUp entirely for Role Type A (OR/Applied Scientist):**
Reviewer A recommends SwagUp disappears entirely for OR/Applied Scientist roles targeting OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind. I disagree with this as a blanket rule. The SwagUp A/B test ($5M+, 40% revenue growth, overturned legacy assumptions, company-wide policy change) demonstrates something the ATLAS and ArXiv stories don't: Danny can identify a problem from data, design an experiment without institutional support, and drive a business decision. That's the entrepreneurial research instinct that AI labs value. Dropping SwagUp entirely for dream-tier targets removes the only story that shows Danny operates independently outside of a large institution. One SwagUp bullet — the A/B test — should survive even for AI lab applications, positioned as evidence of independent problem identification and experimental initiative.

**Reviewer D (Market Benchmarker) — Danny is "top 10-15% of the applicant pool" for Tier 1:**
Reviewer D places Danny in the top 10-15% for Google/Apple. I think this undersells him for a specific, narrow band of roles. For roles that explicitly require the combination of optimization/OR expertise AND experimentation AND independent research, Danny is closer to top 3-5%. The problem is that most DS roles don't require all three — they require one or two. Danny's positioning challenge isn't that he's not strong enough; it's that his superpower is a combination that most JDs aren't explicitly asking for. The resume's job is to make that combination legible as a unified identity, not to make Danny look like a generalist who happens to have done all three things. If the resume succeeds at that, his callback rate at Tier 1 should be higher than 10-15% implies.

**Reviewer G (Layout Strategist) — Education before Experience:**
Reviewer G recommends Education above Experience in the section order. I disagree. Danny has 5 YOE and two ArXiv preprints. His MS is in progress (December 2026). Putting Education first signals "student with internships," not "senior DS with a research practice." The MS credential is important but it belongs below Experience, where it adds to an already-established professional identity rather than leading it. The only exception would be if Danny is applying specifically to research scientist roles at labs where the degree is a hard filter — in that case, the MS (Expected December 2026, Georgia Tech) could move up, but still below the header, not before Experience.

**Reviewer H (Recruiter/ATS) — impact bullet should always lead the JPMC section:**
Reviewer H recommends the $9B impact bullet should always be first, regardless of role type. I partially disagree. For OR/Applied Scientist roles (the formulation-depth audience), leading with the $9B before explaining what the system IS creates a credibility problem — the reader doesn't yet know what generated $9B. For this specific audience, the system description must precede the impact claim. Reviewer H's recommendation is correct for product DS, decision science, and analyst roles, but not for OR/Applied Scientist roles where the formulation IS the credential.
